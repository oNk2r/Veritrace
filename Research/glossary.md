# Glossary

## ESG

ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance) reporting focuses on how climate change and societal factors pose financial risks or opportunities to a business.

## Sustainability Report

Sustainability Reports take a broader perspective, focusing on the company's impacts on society, local communities, and the environment.

## Greenwashing

The practice of making misleading, exaggerated, or unsubstantiated claims about the environmental benefits of a product, service, or corporate strategy.

## Climate TRACE

Climate TRACE (Tracking Real-Time Atmospheric Carbon Emissions) is an independent, non-profit coalition co-founded by Al Gore, clean-tech companies, and academic institutions.

## Greenhouse Gas (GHG)

Gases in the atmosphere that trap heat. When sunlight reaches Earth, the surface absorbs energy and radiates it back as infrared heat. GHGs absorb this infrared radiation and re-emit it, preventing it from escaping into space.

## CO₂e

(Carbon Dioxide Equivalent): A metric used to standardise all different greenhouse gases into a single number based on their Global Warming Potential (GWP).

## Scope 1

Direct Emissions

Emissions from sources owned or directly controlled by the organization.
e.g: Fuel burned in company vehicles, boilers, or machinery; direct gas leaks from refrigeration systems.

## Scope 2

Indirect (Purchased Energy)

Emissions from the generation of purchased energy consumed by the organization.
e.g: Electricity, steam, heating, or cooling bought from a local utility to power an office building or factory.

## Scope 3

Value Chain (Upstream & Downstream)

All other indirect emissions throughout the extended value chain (often 80–90% of a company's total footprint).
e.g: Upstream: Raw material extraction, business travel, employee commuting.

Downstream: Customer use of sold products, product end-of-life disposal.
